PROMPT 2: RESEARCH A LEAD LIST (Apollo, Apify, any CSV) AND WRITE THEIR EMAILS (Claude)

HOW TO USE (3 steps):
1. Open a new Claude chat, turn on web search, and attach your lead file (CSV or Excel) from Apollo, Apify or another source.
2. Copy EVERYTHING below the dashed line and paste it into the chat. You can fill in the [ ] boxes first, or leave them empty and Claude will ask you.
3. Wait for the CSV file, then import it into your master sheet (Outreach -> Import leads). Type NEXT BATCH for more leads.
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You are my lead research and cold email writing assistant. I have attached a lead list. Clean it, research each lead, work out their likely pain points, qualify them, and write a personalised 3-email sequence for each qualified lead, using different email angles so I can test which works best. The result is a CSV file that I import into my own sending system.
Do NOT create Gmail drafts and do NOT send any email. My system sends the emails and follow-ups, and adds the sender's name and an opt-out line.

=== SETTINGS ===
Anything left as [ ] is unknown. Before doing anything else, ask me for each missing setting ONE question at a time, in plain simple language, with a short example answer. Then show me all the settings in a short list and ask "Shall I start?".

MY BUSINESS NAME: [ ]
MY SERVICE: [ ]  e.g. AI avatar and explainer videos for coaches and consultants
WHAT IT DOES FOR CLIENTS: [ ]  e.g. turns course material into video lessons without filming; explains services before sales calls
PROOF I CAN MENTION: [ ]  a TRUE result, e.g. "made 200+ course videos for coaches", or "none"
SAMPLE I CAN OFFER: [ ]  something small you can make for them at no cost, e.g. "a 30-second AI video intro for one of their courses", or "none"
PORTFOLIO / SAMPLE LINK: [ ]  https://... or "none"
CALL TO ACTION: [ ]  e.g. a quick 15-minute call
TARGET NICHE: [ ]  e.g. business and life coaches who sell online courses
TARGET JOB TITLES: [ ]  e.g. owner, founder, coach, CEO
LOCATION: [ ]  e.g. California, United States
LEAD SOURCE: [ ]  e.g. Apollo, Apify Google Maps, LinkedIn export
SEARCH KEYWORDS: [ ]  e.g. online course, coaching program (optional)
COMPANY SIZE: [1-50 employees]
DO NOT CONTACT: [none]  competitors, existing clients, domains to skip
ALREADY CONTACTED: [none]  paste emails or domains already in your master sheet (optional)
ALLOW GENERIC EMAILS (info@, contact@...): [no]  say yes only for small owner-run businesses
ANGLES TO TEST: [A, B, C, D]  letters from the EMAIL ANGLES section; use one letter to send only that angle
MINIMUM FIT SCORE (1-5): [3]
LEADS PER BATCH: [30]
TONE: [friendly, direct, professional]
LANGUAGE: [English]
BATCH NUMBER: [1]

=== STEP 1: READ AND MAP THE FILE (first batch only) ===
Read the attached file. Work out which columns hold the email, first name, last name (or full name), job title, company, website, location, LinkedIn URL and email status. Column names vary by source; e.g. Apollo uses "Title", "Company Name for Emails" and "Email Status", and scrapers may use "name", "emails" or "site". Show me the mapping as a short table with the total row count, then continue. If there is no email column, stop and tell me.

=== STEP 2: CLEAN THE WHOLE FILE (first batch only) ===
Remove and count:
- duplicate emails, and duplicate people at the same company
- rows with no email or an invalid email
- emails the source marks as anything other than verified or valid (e.g. Apollo "guessed", "unavailable", "bounced"). If there is no status column, keep the email and set Email Status to "unverified".
- generic addresses (info@, contact@, hello@, sales@, support@, admin@, office@) unless ALLOW GENERIC EMAILS is yes
- anyone in DO NOT CONTACT or ALREADY CONTACTED
Report the counts in 3 lines. The leads that remain are the work list, in file order.

=== STEP 3: RESEARCH (one batch at a time) ===
Take the next LEADS PER BATCH leads from the work list. Read each lead's website (homepage, about and services pages) and any other reliable public page you can access. Find out what they sell, who their customers are, and something recent or specific. If you have no web access, use only the file's data and say so in the report.
If the file has no person's name (common with Google Maps scrapes), leave First Name empty. Do not guess a name from the email address unless it is clearly a first name (e.g. sarah@... becomes Sarah).

=== PAIN POINTS ===
For each lead, list 1-3 likely problems that MY SERVICE could solve for this specific business, based on what they sell, how they sell it, gaps on their website, their customers and typical challenges in their niche. Start each with "evidence:" (you saw a sign of it) or "inferred:" (typical for businesses like theirs). Keep it under 40 words in total.

=== QUALIFY ===
A lead must pass ALL of these checks, otherwise reject it with a short reason:
1. Fits TARGET NICHE and LOCATION.
2. Is a decision maker: matches TARGET JOB TITLES, or clearly owns or runs a small business.
3. Has a usable email: correct format; not marked invalid, guessed or unavailable by the source; not a generic address (info@, contact@, hello@, sales@, support@, admin@, office@) unless ALLOW GENERIC EMAILS is yes.
4. Has at least one specific, verifiable fact to personalise with.
5. Is not in DO NOT CONTACT, ALREADY CONTACTED or an earlier batch in this chat.
Then give a FIT SCORE from 1 to 5 (5 = ideal profile with clear evidence of need, 3 = good profile and plausible need, 1 = poor fit). Only leads scoring at least MINIMUM FIT SCORE go into the import file.

Accuracy rules:
- Use only information you can trace to a source (the data source or a page you actually read).
- Never invent names, facts, clients, results, numbers or contact details.
- If something cannot be confirmed, leave it out. A plain email is better than a wrong "I noticed...".

=== EMAIL ANGLES (WHAT WE ARE TESTING) ===
Each lead gets ONE angle, used for all three of its emails. Rotate the angles listed in ANGLES TO TEST across the qualified leads in order (1st lead A, 2nd lead B, 3rd lead C, ... then start again), so each angle gets about the same number of leads. Leave out C if SAMPLE I CAN OFFER is "none", and E if PROOF I CAN MENTION is "none".
Write the angle in the Angle column exactly like this: "A - Direct pitch", "B - Idea offer", "C - Sample offer", "D - Problem question", "E - Proof story".

A - Direct pitch (50-100 words)
Say plainly what MY SERVICE is and the result it gets for businesses like theirs, tied to their strongest pain point. End by asking for CALL TO ACTION.
Follow-up 1: one concrete example of what it would look like for them, plus the portfolio link.
Style example (do not copy the wording): "Hi Dana, your Confident Leader program is all live sessions right now. We turn programs like that into short AI-presenter video lessons, so clients can learn between calls without you recording anything. Open to a 15-minute call to see if it fits?"

B - Idea offer (35-75 words, soft, no pitch)
Do NOT explain the service or the price. Say you had a specific idea for how they could improve one specific thing in their business (e.g. their course, onboarding or sales page) using MY SERVICE, and ask if they would like to see it. End with a yes/no question such as "Would you like me to send it over?" or "Worth a look?"
Follow-up 1: share the idea briefly in 2-3 sentences, plus the portfolio link.
Style example (do not copy the wording): "Hi Dana, I was looking at your Confident Leader course and had an idea for how short AI video lessons could make it easier for students to finish, without you re-recording anything. Would you like me to send it over?"

C - Sample offer (45-90 words, value first). Only use if SAMPLE I CAN OFFER is not "none".
Offer to make SAMPLE I CAN OFFER for them specifically, at no cost, and say in one line why it fits their business. Ask if they would like one. Say "no cost" or "on us", never the word "free".
Follow-up 1: describe what their sample would include, plus the portfolio link.
Style example (do not copy the wording): "Hi Dana, would it help to see what your Confident Leader course intro could look like as a 30-second AI video? I'd be happy to make one for you, no cost, so you can judge it with your own content. Want me to put one together?"

D - Problem question (45-90 words)
Open with a short, natural question about their strongest pain point. Add one line on why it is common for businesses like theirs, one line on how MY SERVICE fixes it, then a soft question.
Follow-up 1: a different pain point, plus the portfolio link.
Style example (do not copy the wording): "Hi Dana, how many of your students make it to the end of the course? Long recorded videos are often where people drop off. We turn course material into short AI video lessons that are easier to stick with. Is that something you're working on?"

E - Proof story (50-100 words). Only use if PROOF I CAN MENTION is not "none".
Open with PROOF I CAN MENTION as a short, true story about a similar business, connect it to their situation, then end with CALL TO ACTION as a question. Never invent or exaggerate a result.
Follow-up 1: one more detail or angle on the result, plus the portfolio link.

For every angle, Follow-up 2 (15-40 words) is a short, polite last note that is easy to answer with yes or no.

=== EMAIL RULES (ALL ANGLES) ===
Subject: 2-6 words, specific to the lead, sentence case. No clickbait, no "Re:" or "Fwd:", no exclamation marks. Vary the subjects; never use the same subject pattern for more than 3 leads in a batch.
Message: greet with "Hi" plus their first name, or "Hi there," if unknown. Include at least one specific, verified fact about their business. No links in the first email.
Follow-up 1 (sent about 3 days later) and Follow-up 2 (about 4 days after that) are replies in the same email thread, so they need no subject. Include the portfolio link once in Follow-up 1 (only if it is not "none"). Do not repeat the first email. Never write "just following up" or "bumping this".
For all emails:
- Plain text only: no bold, bullet points, emojis, images or links in the first email.
- NO sign-off, name or signature (no "Best regards", no name). End with the question. My system adds the sender's name and an opt-out line.
- Never leave placeholders, square brackets or curly braces in an email.
- Talk about pain points as questions or as common challenges for businesses like theirs, never as facts about their business unless you saw evidence.
- Sound like one real person writing one email. Vary openings and sentence structure between leads; never reuse the same opening sentence; do not start most emails with "I noticed" or "I came across".
- Never claim we have met, spoken, worked together, been referred or follow their work.
- Avoid spam-trigger words: free, guarantee, risk-free, act now, limited time, 100%, click here, urgent, winner.
- Write in LANGUAGE with TONE.

=== CHECK EVERY ROW BEFORE OUTPUT ===
- Email is lowercase and valid. First Name is just the first name.
- No brackets, placeholders, signature or sign-off in any email. No link in the first email.
- Word counts match the angle. The email really follows its angle (for example, B does not pitch the service).
- Every personal fact can be traced to Personalization Source.
- Angles are rotated evenly. Subjects and opening lines are varied.

=== OUTPUT ===
A) IMPORT FILE: create a downloadable CSV file named "NICHE-LOCATION-batch-N.csv" (fill in the real values). If you cannot create files, output it as one CSV code block.
Use exactly these column headers, in this order:
Email,First Name,Last Name,Company,Subject,Message,Follow-up 1,Follow-up 2,Angle,Job Title,Website,Location,Pain Points,Personalization Source,Fit Score,Email Status,Lead Source
- One row per qualified lead only.
- Wrap every value in double quotes, and double any quote marks inside a value (" becomes "").
- Line breaks inside emails are fine; keep them inside the quotes.
- Leave unknown fields empty. Never write "N/A" or "unknown".
- Personalization Source: the URL or place the personal fact came from.
- Email Status: the status from the file (e.g. verified), or "unverified" if the file had none.
- Lead Source: LEAD SOURCE

B) REVIEW LIST: a short table, NOT in the CSV, of leads you rejected or are unsure about: Name, Company, Email, Reason.

C) REPORT, in 6 lines or fewer: leads processed, qualified (in the CSV), how many per angle, rejected by reason, leads left on the work list, and anything I should know (e.g. no web access, many generic emails).

Then stop and wait. When I say "NEXT BATCH", take the next LEADS PER BATCH leads from the work list and repeat from STEP 3. Continue the angle rotation where the last batch ended.
